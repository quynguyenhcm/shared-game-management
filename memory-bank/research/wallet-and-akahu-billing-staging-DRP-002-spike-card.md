### TRAP Candidates
- Windcave A2A and Worldline Online EFTPOS are merchant-of-record models: the payment settles into the registered merchant's own account. Adopting either as-is would force one merchant onboarding per team, directly contradicting ADR 0016 (no per-team merchant onboarding). Candidate disqualifier unless the provider confirms a payee-override or facilitator arrangement.
- Platform-first settlement (BlinkPay/Windcave/Worldline with ONE Argus merchant) turns the platform into a funds holder paying teams out later. That is a money-handling/regulatory and ledger obligation (PSP/AML questions), not just an API choice. Flagged only; compliance review was not performed.
- BlinkPay's one-off debit is triggered by the first server-side `getQuickPayment()` after authorisation, so the confirm path is not purely webhook-driven (candidate conflict with AC-ASYNC-3 webhook-then-re-read idempotency design and with P5 no-call-under-lock).
- BlinkPay documented webhook events are recurring-payment events only; one-off event names and payloads are not publicly listed. Fixture-building risk.
- Windcave and Qippay publish no public developer contract for A2A/PayBy; evidence for AC-INTEGRATION-1 would be invented or sales-gated.
- Akahu webhook payload has no unique event id and no state field; dedupe must be built from (`_payment`, `status`, `emitted_at`) or by always re-reading. (Not an alternative-provider issue; carried here as a comparison note.)
- POLi relies on screen scraping and its banks' T&Cs warnings (Consumer NZ); not a safe payee-routing rail.

### Open Questions
- Does Akahu permit a platform to initiate one-off payments to arbitrary third-party payee accounts it does not own (marketplace/facilitator terms, approval lead time)? Public docs are silent (DRP-001 owns this; this probe's conclusion is conditional on it).
- Does Akahu's `FAILED`/`REJECTED`/`BANK_ERROR` surface "destination account rejected" distinguishably, and can the sandbox (`demo_bank`) produce it? (DRP-001.)
- BlinkPay: does a payee/destination or facilitator model exist, such that one platform merchant can pay many third-party BECS accounts? Needs a direct BlinkPay conversation (support contact is listed in the PHP SDK package page). Settlement timing "about 2 hours" implies settlement through BlinkPay, i.e. platform-first.
- Windcave: is there a public A2A developer spec, per-transaction fees, and any sub-merchant/payee model? Sales-gated.
- Worldline Online EFTPOS: can a Root Parent account represent a platform with Child accounts per payee without individual merchant agreements (i.e. a facilitator/PF model)? Fees and approval timeline not public.
- Qippay (PayBy) and Volley: public docs insufficient; do they offer a payee-per-payment API? (Volley powering Givealittle suggests multi-beneficiary use, fund flow undisclosed.)
- Live PISP list for 2025-2026 (API Centre register): not retrieved; the only list found is dated 2024-05-30 (OUT-OF-WINDOW).
- Is there an "NZ open banking rules take effect 1 Dec 2025" regulatory change (CDR-style designation) that alters PISP accreditation or fee caps for platforms? One press-release mention only, unverified.
- Whether holding customer funds on behalf of teams even for T+0/T+1 triggers registration obligations in NZ (FSP Act) if a settle-to-platform provider is chosen. Not researched.

### Distilled conclusion for the orchestrator (evidence summary, not a verdict)
- Akahu's one-off payments API, as publicly documented, already takes an arbitrary third-party payee (name + BECS account number) per request from a payer who does not connect an account, authenticated by platform app token + secret. This lowers the need for any alternative.
- No alternative provider found publicly documents a per-payment payee for one platform credential. Windcave, Worldline and BlinkPay are merchant/settlement models; Qippay and Volley disclose too little. Any fallback would likely mean platform-first settlement or per-team merchants, both in tension with ADR 0016.
- Of the alternatives, Worldline has the most mature published contract (signed JWT + JWKS, statuses, polling guidance) and BlinkPay the most developer-friendly self-serve onboarding (and an open SDK), but both need vendor confirmation of a payee/facilitator model.
