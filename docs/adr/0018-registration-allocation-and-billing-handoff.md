## Context

Parties of one to three (a registrant plus up to two guests) share a session's public capacity (`max_players - reserved_seats`) and are confirmed or waitlisted whole. Workflows section 2 makes a late withdrawer free when someone takes their place and liable otherwise. Payments can be created only by wallet-and-akahu-billing at session end (an Akahu link, a GST snapshot, line items with a registrant line; ADR 0012), so this feature must record liability without writing a money row.

Four earlier decisions bind this one:

- ADR 0015 (membership and the ban seam): members are gated with `team_members FOR SHARE` before any session lock, and a ban withdrawal uses early semantics.
- ADR 0016 (team banking): lock order and publish gate. This feature never takes a `teams` lock after a session lock, never publishes, and does not bypass or re-implement the publish gate. A PUBLISHED session is assumed to have passed it. The hand-off list stays money-free.
- ADR 0017 (skill levels and Auto Enroll): C-SKL-1 (the auto-enrol selection and its re-check), C-PRICE-1 (the registrant's pricing attributes are snapshotted) and C-GAME-1 (what a "game" is, which constrains registration rows after completion).
- ADR 0019 (session scheduling engine) offers the allocator port and the contracts C-SSE-1 to C-SSE-5, and hands this feature a list of obligations at its build gate (ADR 0019 sections 6 and 8). That ADR merged first. Where the original creative text and ADR 0019 differ, ADR 0019 and the 2026-10-24 task amendment win, and this ADR says so.

## Decision

### 1. One pure decision, applied under the session lock

Every seat change (register, withdraw, ban withdrawal, capacity change, auto-enrol) calls the pure `decideAllocation` (`apps/api/src/modules/registrations/allocation.ts`) under the D-14 session lock and applies its result through guarded writes in `repo.ts`, which is the only file that writes `registrations` and `registration_guests` (structural test, `boundaries.test.ts`). The decision returns refusals as values. Refusals are never thrown, so one refused member cannot abort a caller's multi-member transaction.

Every operation ends with the single promotion step (`settle` in `allocator.ts`), and `promoteWaitlist` is its exported wrapper, so the first-fit invariant heals itself: after any operation, no WAITLISTED party fits the free seats.

### 2. First-fit promotion (D-14 refined)

When seats are free, WAITLISTED parties are scanned in ascending `queue_position` and every party that fits is promoted, in order. A party that does not fit keeps its position and is offered seats first next time. A newcomer is scanned after the whole waitlist, so it is confirmed only if it still fits. Parties are never split and nobody is demoted. Fairness is FIFO among the parties that fit, not FIFO absolute, and the copy says so. This refines D-14's "promote the lowest position" to "promote, lowest position first, every party that fits". The ledger entry D-14 is unchanged: its locking and enqueue rules hold as written.

A waitlisted member whose membership is no longer APPROVED (banned, and the ban job has not withdrawn them yet) is not promoted. The allocator reads each waitlisted row with its member's status and passes it to the decision marked `eligible: false`: first-fit skips it and promotes the next approved party instead, but the entry stays in the queue, so it still counts toward the tail and the ban handler can withdraw it (`withdrawFromSession({ cause: 'ban' })` finds the row, withdraws it as BANNED from WAITLISTED, no fee). The row keeps its `queue_position` until the ban handler withdraws it, and a new position is always above every position still held, hidden rows included, so the partial unique index `uq_registrations_waitlist_position` cannot refuse an insert.

### 3. Time

One `now` per operation. Registration is open while `status = 'PUBLISHED' AND now < starts_at`. A withdrawal is EARLY iff `now <= cancellation_deadline` (inclusive) and LATE otherwise; a ban is BANNED. Nothing is promoted at or after `starts_at`. The open check runs before the classification. Port calls use `ctx.now`, which the caller read after its own locks (section 8). The module's own transactions read the clock once, before the first attempt.

### 4. Liability and the owed-fee marker (D-62, D-63, D-67)

- A LATE withdrawal of a CONFIRMED party is fee-due iff its own step promoted nobody. A fee-due withdrawal is released, whole and oldest-first, when a later step before `starts_at` confirms seats: the budget is the seats confirmed (a fee-due member re-registering into a CONFIRMED place nets off its own prior party size, because taking that seat back clears its own fee), each release is whole while the budget is above zero, and the current withdrawer is first in its own step. Releases are one-way. EARLY, BANNED and waitlist withdrawals are never fee-due. A promoted party takes the fee by being CONFIRMED. Ban-triggered promotions release owed fees too.
- The marker is `registrations.withdrawal_fee_due boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false`, with `withdrawal_kind` (EARLY, LATE, BANNED) and `withdrawn_at`. It means "a late-withdrawal fee is currently owed". The database lets it become true only in the UPDATE that enters WITHDRAWN with kind LATE, and afterwards only true to false (D-63, D-64). The partial index `(session_id, withdrawn_at) WHERE withdrawal_fee_due` (D-67) serves the oldest-first release read and the widened D-85 predicate (section 7).
- Re-registration reuses the one row `UNIQUE(session_id, user_id)` allows and replaces its guests. While a fee is owed, a re-registration is allowed only into a CONFIRMED place for a group at least as large; otherwise it is refused with 409 `late_withdrawal_fee_due`, and nothing is written.
- An admin capacity cut can leave a liability standing for a seat that no longer exists. The admin chose fewer places and the player did withdraw late. The rule is uniform and is noted for review with billing and admin reporting.
- O7 (human ruling 2026-10-26): the all-or-nothing release above is the product rule. Any refill of the place releases the oldest liable late fee whole, and rounding favours the player (R2). The per-unfilled-seat alternative (charge the party for each of its seats nobody took) is rejected. Two worked examples:
  - A party of three withdraws late and only one waitlisted player is promoted into the freed seats. The withdrawal promoted somebody, so it is not fee-due: the party owes nothing, although two of its three seats stay empty.
  - A party of three withdraws late with nobody waiting, so it owes the fee. Later, before the start, one new registrant confirms into one of the three seats. That one confirmed seat is a budget above zero, so the whole fee is released, although two seats stay empty.

### 5. The registrant's pricing snapshot (D-62, D-63; ADR 0017 C-PRICE-1, R15)

`registrations.registrant_skill_level` and `registrations.registrant_sex` are written by `repo.ts` in the same statement that makes a row CONFIRMED, on all four transitions into CONFIRMED (register, promotion, auto-enrol, a confirmed re-registration). They are read from `team_members.skill_level` and `users.sex`. Register and auto-enrol read them under the `team_members FOR SHARE` lock the member gate already holds. A promotion reads `team_members.skill_level` with an unlocked subselect (`repo.ts:460-486`), and `users.sex` is never locked on any transition. Plain reads are enough: the value is a snapshot of the member's attributes at the moment of confirmation, and a concurrent edit simply lands on one side of it. A CHECK requires both to be non-NULL when a row is CONFIRMED or its fee is owed, and the transition guard (D-64) forbids changing them except on a transition into CONFIRMED. The fee estimate shown before confirmation uses the current level and names it. The level used for the charge is the one at confirmation.

### 6. Auto-enrol (ADR 0017 C-SKL-1; ADR 0019 section 6)

- `autoEnrolInSession(ctx, { teamId, sessionId, entries })` takes the entries in `selectAutoEnrollees` order. It runs in SSE's follow-up transaction (`runSessionAutoEnrol`), whose first statement is SSE's advisory lock. This replaces the "inside the materialisation transaction" wording of the original C-SAE-1 (ARCH line 590).
- The gate step runs `team_members FOR SHARE` for every entry, in caller order, before the one session lock. Under that row lock the allocator re-checks `is_auto_enroll`, `auto_enroll_until IS NULL OR auto_enroll_until >= game_sessions.date`, and `auto_enroll_assigned_at = entry.assignedAt` at millisecond precision. A mismatch is the returned skip `auto_enroll_changed` (R16).
- Members with three or more OVERDUE payments, or no longer APPROVED, are skipped without a row. A party never has guests.
- An existing row in ANY status, WITHDRAWN of any kind included, is skipped as `already_registered`, checked under the session lock after the member gates and before the decision. The row is not re-activated, relabelled or emailed (ADR 0019 section 8 (b), ALG R-b, R21). A retried durable job, or a sweep after a member withdrew, therefore never puts them back. `late_withdrawal_fee_due` cannot arise here, so it is not a port reason.
- Placements are labelled `source = 'AUTO_ENROL'` (D-62) and one email is queued in the placing transaction per placed member, never for a skip (key `auto-enrol:<registrationId>:<registered_at ISO>`), unless the session is in the O8 backlog (below). A panel register or re-register is always `MEMBER`.
- O5 (human ruling 2026-10-25): once a session's free-cancellation deadline has passed (`now > game_sessions.cancellation_deadline`), auto-enrol adds nobody, neither CONFIRMED nor WAITLISTED, because a member placed after the deadline would owe a late fee for withdrawing and the email could only tell them to withdraw before a date already gone. The check runs under the session lock against the locked row (a deadline edit may have committed since the header read), so `now == deadline` still enrols, matching `classifyWithdrawal`. Every member of the call is skipped with the port reason `deadline_passed`, logged once each as `registration.refused`. It covers every auto-enrol path (the `session-auto-enrol` job, the recurrence sweep, the boot sweep, and capacity-edit or series-propagation calls through the port). The deadline check runs before the `already_registered` check, so after the deadline a member who already has a row is reported as `deadline_passed`; both are skips and nobody is placed. SSE still stamps the D-80 marker (`auto_enrolled_at`) in the same transaction, so the sweep does not retry the session. Consequence: if an admin later moves the deadline forward, the stamped session is not re-offered to auto-enrol; those members register by hand. `deadline_passed` is in the shared `AllocationRefusalReason` but is never an HTTP error: manual registration after the deadline is still allowed, and waitlist promotion is unaffected.
- P-1 (ADR 0019 section 6): Auto Enroll applies to every published session, one-off or series. On the first worker boot after the real adapter is wired (section 9), SSE's boot sweep enrols every PUBLISHED, not-started session that was left pending. ADR 0019 accepts that enrolment as the bounded exception to ADR 0017 point 7. Sessions already past their deadline (starting within 48 h by default) are skipped by O5 and only stamped. The one-email-per-member-per-session burst this run would have sent is removed by O8.
- O8 (human ruling 2026-10-26, D-86): the pre-deploy backlog is enrolled silently. The migration `20261026000100000_auto_enrol_silent_backlog.sql` adds `game_sessions.auto_enrol_notify boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT true` and sets it false on every session that is PUBLISHED, `auto_enrol_required` and `auto_enrolled_at IS NULL` when it runs, which is exactly the backlog SSE left before registration shipped. `runSessionAutoEnrol` reads the flag with its due check and passes it to the port as the required input `notify`; `autoEnrolInSession` with `notify: false` places, labels (`AUTO_ENROL`), snapshots and logs (`registration.created`) exactly as usual and SSE stamps the D-80 marker, but no R17 email is queued. Members see "Added automatically (Auto Enroll)" on the session page as usual. `session.auto_enrol.completed` carries `silent` (the number placed without an email; a count, never ids or addresses) only for such a session. Every session published after the migration has the default true and emails as normal. The only production caller of the port method is `runSessionAutoEnrol` (the inline publish follow-up, the `session-auto-enrol` job, the recurrence sweep and the boot sweep all go through it); capacity edits and series propagation call `promoteWaitlist` only. The flag is bookkeeping: the migration re-creates `game_sessions_version_bump` under the same name with `auto_enrol_notify` added to its TG_ARGV list (beside the D-80 columns), so the backfill bumps neither `version` nor `updated_at` and an admin edit form opened before the deploy is not refused as stale. Nothing in the application writes the flag after the migration.

### 7. D-85: the fee lock, the display count and the O1 split (D-68)

ADR 0019 section 8 (a) asks this feature to widen SSE's predicate `game_session_has_active_registrations` to also count a WITHDRAWN row with `withdrawal_fee_due`, so a late fee that is still owed keeps a session's fees locked. The migration does this with `CREATE OR REPLACE` (D-68). Its down migration restores SSE's original body and never drops the function, because `game_sessions_fee_raise_locked` depends on it. The TypeScript twin `countActiveRegistrations` in `apps/api/src/modules/sessions/repo.ts` widens in the same change, and the parity test `sessions-parity.int.test.ts` fails if the two drift (AC-VERIFY-12).

**O1 ruling (human, 2026-10-24): split.** One count cannot serve both jobs:

- `countActiveRegistrations` (the fee lock) counts CONFIRMED, WAITLISTED and fee-due WITHDRAWN rows. It drives only `feesLocked`, the 409 `session_fee_locked`, and the series `fee_locked` skip.
- `countRegisteredPlayers` (display) counts only CONFIRMED and WAITLISTED rows, one per registration. It drives every "{n} players are registered" line: the edit and fee-lock banners, the cancel and end-series counts, and the list and series rows.

The rejected alternatives were one widened count for everything, which would tell an admin that "1 player is registered" when that person withdrew and merely owes a fee, and seat counting with guests, which was not adopted. Every SSE call site is classified once in the task file (R20 table) and was switched in Phase 1. One copy edge is left to SSE: a session whose only liable row is a fee-due withdrawal shows "No one has registered yet." beside the fee-lock message. The fee-lock wording was reworded to "a player has registered or still owes a late-withdrawal fee", and the series lines follow it.

### 8. Contracts honoured, and the lock order inside SSE's transactions

**The lock order** is ADR 0019's `LOCK_ORDER` (`apps/api/src/modules/sessions/locks.ts`):

```
teams -> recurring_session_rules -> team_members -> game_sessions -> registrations -> registration_guests -> payments
```

Registration code uses a slice of it. It never locks `recurring_session_rules`, never takes a `teams` lock (so never after a session lock, ADR 0016), takes `team_members` only `FOR SHARE` through the gate and before any session lock, takes `game_sessions` only `FOR UPDATE` (D-14, one row per transaction), and registrations and guests after that. It takes no advisory lock: `SESSION_AUTO_ENROL_LOCK_NAMESPACE` (`0x5345_4153`) is SSE's alone and must not be reused here. READ COMMITTED throughout. `registerInSession` reads the debt count with no lock, then gates the member, then locks the session; `withdrawFromSession` and `promoteWaitlist` take the session lock first and no member lock. A deferred capacity trigger (D-65) is the database backstop: it takes the session row `FOR UPDATE NOWAIT`, so a writer that skipped the allocator fails 55P03 at once instead of forming a registration-to-session wait cycle, and it never blocks a capacity cut or a withdrawal.

**Port calls run in the caller's transaction** (R22). The adapter `createRegistrationsSessionAllocator` (`apps/api/src/modules/registrations/session-allocator-adapter.ts`) is a thin wrapper. It adds `name: 'registrations'`, `enrols: true`, the `trx.isTransaction` assertion and an exhaustive mapping of results to the port's types. It opens no transaction, sets no `lock_timeout`, does not retry and reads no clock. SSE's policies apply: its HTTP policy (2 s, three attempts) for the session `PUT` and its job policy (5 s, three attempts) for the follow-up. Every port call is safe to run again when SSE retries the whole transaction, because a retried attempt starts from rolled-back state (proved with an injected deadlock in `registrations-adapter.int.test.ts`). The module's own policies (R5: 2 s HTTP, 5 s job, three attempts, `now` read once) apply only to this module's own transactions (register, withdraw, the ban handler). The adapter imports only `allocator-port.ts` from the sessions module (`boundaries.test.ts`).

### 9. The adapter and its wiring (R19; ADR 0019 section 8)

The port is SSE's (`allocator-port.ts`), the adapter is this feature's, and `createSessionAllocator` (`apps/api/src/session-allocator.ts`) stays the single composition point. Phase 2 builds the adapter and proves it by injecting it into SSE's own code paths: `runSessionAutoEnrol`, the session `PUT` and series propagation (`registrations-adapter.int.test.ts`). **The swap landed in Phase 3.** The body of `createSessionAllocator` now returns `createRegistrationsSessionAllocator(createAllocator({ jobs, webBaseUrl }))` in place of the no-op, and `session-allocator.test.ts` proves both entrypoints call it. The swap waited for Phase 3 so that the branch never enrols anyone before both the promotion email and the auto-enrol email exist. No environment variable selects the adapter. `AppDeps.sessionAllocator` and `RegisterJobsOptions.sessionAllocator` are already required, and `jobs` is the API producer or the worker runner, so emails go on the caller's transaction either way. The real-adapter marker proof (a pending session enrolled once and stamped, a second sweep enrolling nobody) is AC-INTEGRATION-6, whose domain half is Phase 2 and whose end-to-end half is Phase 3.

### 10. Route ownership, O2 and O3 (R23, R24)

**Route namespace.** Reserved for this feature: `GET /teams/:teamId/sessions/upcoming` and `/teams/:teamId/sessions/:sessionId/registration*`. SSE's `GET /teams/:teamId/sessions/:sessionId` is `TEAM_ADMIN`, so the static `upcoming` segment must win over SSE's parametric route (tested in Phase 3). The player page `/teams/[teamId]/sessions/[sessionId]` and the "Upcoming sessions" section are this feature's. SSE's admin pages stay under `/teams/[teamId]/manage/sessions`, and only the registration panel changes a registration.

**O2 ruling (human, 2026-10-24): another team's session id stays 404 on registration's routes.** ADR 0019 (e) answers 403 `forbidden` for an id that exists under another team and 404 for an id that exists nowhere, using a lock-free `SELECT id` by id alone on the failure path of SSE's admin routes. This feature's member-facing routes answer 404 `not_found` with an identical body for another team's id, a DRAFT id and an unknown id (AC-ENTRY-2). The difference is deliberate. SSE's routes are `TEAM_ADMIN` routes: a caller who administers a team may be told an id exists elsewhere. A member-facing route is reachable by every member, and a 403 would tell a member that a session id exists in a team they cannot see. The 404 rule was approved with the spec. Registration's service therefore never calls `sessionExists` or any lookup by id alone: every read is scoped by `(sessionId, teamId)`. A test in `registrations-api.int.test.ts` (Phase 3) asserts the identical 404 body for all three cases. The O2 check runs BEFORE the member gate (`access.ts`, through the allocator): the lock-free team-scoped `readSessionHeader` in the register service answers 404 for an unknown, DRAFT or other-team id, so a debtor who owes three or more payments learns nothing extra about a session they cannot see.

**Read-model `blockedReason` precedence (Phase 3).** `GET .../registration` reports the first reason that stops a new registration, in this order: `session_not_open`, `already_registered`, `overdue_payments` (3 or more), `party_exceeds_capacity` (no public place). A fee-due withdrawn caller is not blocked here; whether the group fits is the allocator's answer. The POST keeps its own order (the member gate first, so a debtor gets 403 `overdue_payments` even on a closed session), which is why the page and the POST can differ for a closed session with debt. The rule lives in `access.ts`. A consequence, accepted: a debtor who POSTs to a visible closed session (PUBLISHED but started, or CANCELLED or COMPLETED) gets 403 `overdue_payments`, while the read model reports `session_not_open` for the same session. The POST keeps the member gate first, so the difference is a known page/POST difference, not a bug.

**Player web (Phase 5).** The player page `apps/web/src/app/teams/[teamId]/sessions/[sessionId]/page.tsx` is registration's, and it makes one read, `GET .../registration`. That single read model feeds both `PlayerSessionSummary` and `RegistrationPanel`, and the panel never fetches again. `PlayerSessionSummary` is deliberately not the admin `SessionSummary` in `components/teams`: the two render different reads with different audiences (R23). Only `RegistrationPanel` changes a registration, through the register and withdraw Server Actions in `lib/registrations/actions.ts`. The panel's controls are conveniences; the API enforces every rule. The withdraw confirmation reuses `ConfirmDisclosure` from `components/teams/confirm-disclosure.tsx`.

**Static route precedence (Phase 3).** `registrationsRoutes` is registered before `sessionsRoutes` in `app.ts`. `upcoming` is a static segment and wins over SSE's `/:sessionId` regardless, and the registration routes are the only ones under `/:sessionId/registration*`. The order records intent; the test in Phase 3 guards it.

**O3 ruling (human, 2026-10-24): accept and document; no `expectedSessionVersion`.** The register body carries no session-version field, and there is no 409 `session_changed`. Before the first registration an admin can still raise fees or change times, so a player can register on terms that differ from the page they loaded. The trade-off is bounded: the window is only the first registrant, or the first few, racing an admin edit. Once anyone is registered, or a late withdrawer still owes a fee, D-85 stops fee raises (`game_sessions_fee_raise_locked`, 409 `session_fee_locked`). The fee line on the page is an estimate. A deadline that moves while the withdraw confirmation is open is covered separately: the withdraw body is strict `{ expectLate: boolean }`, and a LATE classification inside the transaction with `expectLate` false is refused with 409 `cancellation_terms_changed` (R9), so the page re-opens the confirmation with the late wording. Not adopted from ADR 0019's conventions, because they do not fit these routes: `feesLocked` (an admin edit-form concern), explicit `changed` flags on write responses (a repeat register or withdraw is a 409 `already_registered` or `not_registered`), and `expectedVersion` (no registration route edits a versioned row).

### 11. The ban handler and its sweep (ARCH Q4, R6, O4)

**The handler (as built, `modules/registrations/ban-handler.ts`, queue `member-banned`).** `createRegistrationsBanHandler` returns the `MemberBannedHandler` named `registrations`. Its dependencies are `db`, the worker's job runner (`jobs`), `clock` and `webBaseUrl`. It reads `now` once and uses that value to select the member's CONFIRMED and WAITLISTED rows in the team's PUBLISHED sessions with `starts_at > now`, ordered by `(starts_at, id)`. The selection is by current state, so a redelivery selects nothing. For each session it runs one transaction under `JOB_POLICY` through `runAllocatorTransaction`, which calls `withdrawFromSession` with `cause: 'ban'`. The withdrawal takes the D-14 session lock, withdraws the row as BANNED with no fee, and runs the shared promotion step.

- A refusal from the allocator (the session started or was cancelled, or the row was already withdrawn) is a skip, not a failure. It is logged as `registration.ban.session_skipped` with the ids and the refusal reason.
- A failure on one session is logged as `registration.ban.session_failed` with the ids, the class and the SQLSTATE only, never the error message, because a database message can echo row data. The loop then continues with the next session, so one bad session never holds back the rest.
- Shutdown is checked between sessions. An abort throws a plain error, which is classed as transient, so the retry picks up what is still active.

**Classification.** Transient means SQLSTATE 40P01 (deadlock), 55P03 (lock not available), 40001 (serialisation failure), 57014 (statement timeout), 53300 (too many connections) or class 08 (connection exception). It also covers an abort and any unknown error, because a retry of a harmless unknown costs seconds while giving up on a curable one leaves a ban stuck until the next sweep. Permanent means SQLSTATE class 23 (integrity or constraint violation) or a `RegistrationInvariantError`, where a retry would fail the same way. After the loop the handler throws a plain `Error` if any failure was transient, so the queue retries with backoff. Sessions already done select nothing on the retry. If every failure was permanent, it throws `NonRetryableJobError`, and the job ends `failed` for an operator.

**The sweep (as built, `jobs/ban-reconcile.ts` over `modules/registrations/reconcile.ts`).** The sweep enumerates and never withdraws. For every BANNED membership that still holds an active registration in a future PUBLISHED session of its team, it enqueues the same `member-banned` job that a live ban enqueues. It does this through `enqueueMemberBanned` in the team module, so the payload shape and the singleton key `member-banned:<membershipId>` live in one place. The sweep runs in one transaction per run, so a job row never outlives a failed sweep. It reads candidates in keyset pages of 500 ordered by `(banned_at, id)`, not with a single `LIMIT 1000`. A member whose job keeps failing therefore cannot starve the members behind it. A row that cannot make a valid payload (no `banned_by`) is counted as `invalid` and skipped. The run logs `registration.ban_reconcile.completed` with the counts `scanned`, `enqueued`, `alreadyQueued` and `invalid`, and no ids.

**Schedule.** The sweep is registered as the queue and slot `registration-ban-reconcile` at `45 5 * * *` Pacific/Auckland. It moved from 05:30 by ruling O4, because `skill-progression` already holds 05:30 as the last daily slot before this feature, and 05:45 falls after the 02:00 to 02:59 DST window. `enqueueBootSweeps` in `jobs/bootstrap.ts`, which `worker.ts` calls, enqueues the sweep once at every worker boot, so a stuck ban is repaired at the first boot after a deploy. The sweep can also be run on demand with `pnpm run jobs:trigger registration-ban-reconcile`.

**Other facts.** The handler never emails the banned member and never writes `team_members`. The worker pool is 5 connections, up from 2, because a ban handler can hold a connection through a 5 s lock wait. The 5 is a hardcoded literal in `apps/api/src/worker.ts` (`createPool({ max: 5 })`), not a config key; exposing it as an environment variable (for example `WORKER_DATABASE_POOL_MAX`) is a follow-up.

### 12. Rollback order

This feature widens a function SSE owns (D-85, D-68), re-creates SSE's version-bump trigger with one more bookkeeping column (D-86; its down migration restores SSE's exact definition) and, once Phase 3 ships, replaces the allocator body SSE composes. **Rolling session-scheduling-engine back therefore requires rolling this feature back first.** The down migration restores SSE's original `game_session_has_active_registrations` body and never drops it, because SSE's trigger depends on it and SSE's own down migration drops it. In a rollback, roll registration back before SSE, and never the other way round, or the function SSE needs at its own rollback is not in the state its migration expects.

## Alternatives considered

- **Block (strict head-of-line).** In simulation it leaves four to eight times more seats empty while a large party waits, and charges late withdrawers although someone was waiting. Bounded skip has block's costs plus persisted counters and arbitrary knobs. Best-fit packing breaks FIFO among the parties that fit.
- **Liability decided once at withdrawal.** In simulation 69 to 86 percent of liable seats were resold before the start, so they would be charged twice. Settlement at session end inside billing duplicates the rule across features and cannot express partial coverage. Proportional per-seat liability needs arbitrary guest selection and partial payments for about two to three seats per 100 sessions. A liability table or derivation from history gains nothing over a column, because there is one payment per registration, or is fragile.
- **A single widened count for the fee lock and the display (O1 option a).** Rejected as in section 7.
- **Wiring the adapter in Phase 2.** Rejected: the branch would enrol members before the emails exist (section 9).
- **Registration answering 403 for another team's id (O2).** Rejected as in section 10.
- **`expectedSessionVersion` on register (O3).** Rejected as in section 10.
- **A migration-time `DROP` and re-create of the D-85 function.** Rejected: SSE's trigger depends on it.

## Consequences

- A party of three can be passed over by smaller parties. This is by design and the panel explains it.
- A late withdrawer's outcome is provisional until the start. The panel shows the current state, and a withdrawal's cost is an estimate until billing charges it.
- Rounding favours players on mismatched party sizes. Confirmed as the product rule by O7 (section 4, with the worked examples).
- **Accepted correctness gap (C3), accepted by human ruling O6 (2026-10-26) and not fixed in locking: a ban can commit concurrently with a promotion.** The promotion's `team_members` read is a plain join with no lock, because locking `team_members` after the session lock would violate the canonical lock order (ADR 0015, ADR 0016), which this feature does not change. A member banned in that window can be promoted to CONFIRMED and receive one promotion email they should not. The consequence is bounded: the Phase 4 ban handler then withdraws the row with kind BANNED and no fee, and billing reads the hand-off only at or after `starts_at`. That bound holds only if a handler run or a sweep succeeds before the session starts: see the limit in (b) below. The O6 ruling below accepts it and closes the billing side of it.

**Does the reconcile sweep narrow the window? No.** Neither the sweep nor the handler narrows the window in which a banned member can be promoted. A false promotion email can still be sent, because the promotion's `team_members` read is a plain unlocked read and the ban can commit after it. What the two mechanisms bound is the consequence:

- (a) The `member-banned` job is enqueued in the ban's own transaction, so it runs right after the ban commits. Its handler selects by current state, takes the D-14 session lock and re-reads the member's own row. A promotion that commits after the ban but before the handler's lock therefore still ends with the row withdrawn as BANNED (proved in `registrations-ban.int.test.ts`, "the ban/promotion residual", where the handler selects a WAITLISTED row, blocks on a session lock held by an in-flight promotion, and then withdraws the now-CONFIRMED row).
- (b) If the job fails permanently or never runs, the 05:45 sweep, and the boot sweep, re-enqueue it while the member still holds an active registration. A stuck CONFIRMED row therefore lasts at most until the next successful run or the session start, whichever comes first (24 h worst case when a sweep succeeds). Limit: the handler and the sweep select only sessions with `starts_at` in the future, so a banned member who is still CONFIRMED when a session starts (a job that failed permanently or exhausted its retries during an outage, with the session starting before the next successful sweep) is never healed afterwards: the row stays CONFIRMED, but since O6 the hand-off leaves it out of billing (below). Operators watch `team.member.banned.hook_failed` at level error for exactly this. This residual is part of the C3 acknowledgement. A registration or auto-enrol cannot slip past the handler's selection: the member gate takes `team_members FOR SHARE` (`membership.ts:41`), which conflicts with the ban's `UPDATE` (`teams/repo.ts:468`), so the gate and the ban serialise and the gate either sees the ban or commits before it and is then seen by the handler.

**O6 ruling (human, 2026-10-26): both residuals are accepted, with a billing skip.** The two residual gaps, (a) a ban concurrent with a waitlist promotion and (b) a banned member still CONFIRMED (or WITHDRAWN with a late fee owed) at the session start because the ban hook and the reconcile sweep never succeeded before `starts_at`, are accepted as they are; neither is fixed with an extra lock. The mitigation is in the hand-off (C-BIL-1 (bans)): `listBillableRegistrations` leaves out every row whose registrant's membership in the session's team is BANNED with `banned_at <= starts_at`, so neither residual can turn into a charge. A ban after `starts_at` drops nothing, because billing is final at the start. The membership read is a plain unlocked `NOT EXISTS` on `team_members`, for two reasons: locking `team_members` after the session lock would invert the lock order, and no lock is needed for a stable answer, because BANNED is terminal (D-42) and `banned_at` is written once in the statement that bans (D-41). A ban the read does not see commits with `banned_at` equal to its own `now`, which is after `starts_at` for any ban that begins once billing can run. The one remaining edge is a ban whose transaction read its clock before `starts_at` and commits after billing's read, a window of one transaction at the start instant; that member is billed, as billing's read decided. Proved in `registrations-billing-handoff.int.test.ts` (O6: banned before and exactly at the start are excluded, CONFIRMED and fee-due LATE alike; banned after the start is still billed; a ban in another team excludes nothing).

The only unrepaired effect is the one false promotion email. Status of the acknowledgement: given (O6, 2026-10-26).

- **CI flake watch item:** the HTTP race tests in `registrations-http-races.int.test.ts` run through `HTTP_POLICY` (2 s `lock_timeout`, three attempts). On a very slow CI runner, a request blocked behind the test's held session lock could exhaust that policy and surface as 409 or 5xx `lock_retry`. These tests therefore depend on the 2 s `lock_timeout`. They are a flake watch item. They assert that no `registration.lock_retry` log line is emitted, so a slow runner fails loudly rather than passing silently.
- **Deferred optimisation (M2):** `autoEnrolInSession` reloads the session state for each member it places (about six queries per member, the session lock held for the whole batch). Batches are small and each placement changes the state the next one needs, so this is accepted for now; carrying the state forward through the batch is a known, deferred optimisation.
- A banned member's waitlisted row can sit in the queue, holding its position, until the ban handler withdraws it. It is never promoted meanwhile (section 2).
- On the first worker boot after the adapter ships, the boot sweep enrols every pending session (P-1) without emails (O8): the backlog was set `auto_enrol_notify = false` by the D-86 migration, which runs before the new worker starts. Operators see `session.auto_enrol.completed` with a `silent` count per backlog session and no send-email burst. Each session is stamped once, in the transaction that places its members. Sessions published after the migration email as normal.
- Each consumer's plan must cite C-SSE, C-SAE, C-BIL and C-ALL here. The build gate of this feature re-runs `sessions-contracts.int.test.ts` and the D-85 parity test.
- The cancelled-session state is visible only on the page (P-2). A cancelled session with CONFIRMED rows is never billed, because billing bills PUBLISHED to COMPLETED only.
- Two encodings of the transition rules exist (SQL guard and the TypeScript decision). A shared scenario matrix runs against both, so they cannot drift unnoticed.
