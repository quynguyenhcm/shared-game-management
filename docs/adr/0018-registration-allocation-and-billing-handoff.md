## Context

Parties of one to three (a registrant plus up to two guests) share a session's public capacity (`max_players - reserved_seats`) and are confirmed or waitlisted whole. Workflows section 2 makes a late withdrawer free when someone takes their place and liable otherwise. Payments can be created only by wallet-and-akahu-billing at session end (an Akahu link, a GST snapshot, line items with a registrant line; ADR 0012), so this feature must record liability without writing a money row.

Four earlier decisions bind this one:

- ADR 0015 (membership and the ban seam): members are gated with `team_members FOR SHARE` before any session lock, and a ban withdrawal uses early semantics.
- ADR 0016 (team banking): lock order and publish gate. This feature never takes a `teams` lock after a session lock, never publishes, and does not bypass or re-implement the publish gate. A PUBLISHED session is assumed to have passed it. The hand-off list stays money-free.
- ADR 0017 (skill levels and Auto Enroll): C-SKL-1 (the auto-enrol selection and its re-check), C-PRICE-1 (the registrant's pricing attributes are snapshotted) and C-GAME-1 (what a "game" is, which constrains registration rows after completion).
- ADR 0019 (session scheduling engine) offers the allocator port and the contracts C-SSE-1 to C-SSE-5, and hands this feature a list of obligations at its build gate (ADR 0019 sections 6 and 8). That ADR merged first. Where the original creative text and ADR 0019 differ, ADR 0019 and the 2026-10-24 task amendment win, and this ADR says so.

## Decision

### 1. One pure decision, applied under the session lock

Every seat change (register, withdraw, ban withdrawal, capacity change, auto-enrol) calls the pure `decideAllocation` (`apps/api/src/modules/registrations/allocation.ts`) under the D-14 session lock and applies its result through guarded writes in `repo.ts`, which is the only file that writes `registrations` and `registration_guests` (structural test, `boundaries.test.ts`). The decision returns refusals as values. Refusals are never thrown, so one refused member cannot abort a caller's multi-member transaction.

Every operation ends with the single promotion step (`settle` in `allocator.ts`), and `promoteWaitlist` is its exported wrapper, so the first-fit invariant heals itself: after any operation, no WAITLISTED party fits the free seats.

### 2. First-fit promotion (D-14 refined)

When seats are free, WAITLISTED parties are scanned in ascending `queue_position` and every party that fits is promoted, in order. A party that does not fit keeps its position and is offered seats first next time. A newcomer is scanned after the whole waitlist, so it is confirmed only if it still fits. Parties are never split and nobody is demoted. Fairness is FIFO among the parties that fit, not FIFO absolute, and the copy says so. This refines D-14's "promote the lowest position" to "promote, lowest position first, every party that fits". The ledger entry D-14 is unchanged: its locking and enqueue rules hold as written.

A waitlisted member whose membership is no longer APPROVED (banned, and the ban job has not withdrawn them yet) is not promoted. The allocator reads each waitlisted row with its member's status and passes it to the decision marked `eligible: false`: first-fit skips it and promotes the next approved party instead, but the entry stays in the queue, so it still counts toward the tail and the ban handler can withdraw it (`withdrawFromSession({ cause: 'ban' })` finds the row, withdraws it as BANNED from WAITLISTED, no fee). The row keeps its `queue_position` until the ban handler withdraws it, and a new position is always above every position still held, hidden rows included, so the partial unique index `uq_registrations_waitlist_position` cannot refuse an insert.

### 3. Time

One `now` per operation. Registration is open while `status = 'PUBLISHED' AND now < starts_at`. A withdrawal is EARLY iff `now <= cancellation_deadline` (inclusive) and LATE otherwise; a ban is BANNED. Nothing is promoted at or after `starts_at`. The open check runs before the classification. Port calls use `ctx.now`, which the caller read after its own locks (section 8). The module's own transactions read the clock once, before the first attempt.

### 4. Liability and the owed-fee marker (D-62, D-63, D-67)

- A LATE withdrawal of a CONFIRMED party is fee-due iff its own step promoted nobody. A fee-due withdrawal is released, whole and oldest-first, when a later step before `starts_at` confirms seats: the budget is the seats confirmed (a fee-due member re-registering into a CONFIRMED place nets off its own prior party size, because taking that seat back clears its own fee), each release is whole while the budget is above zero, and the current withdrawer is first in its own step. Releases are one-way. EARLY, BANNED and waitlist withdrawals are never fee-due. A promoted party takes the fee by being CONFIRMED. Ban-triggered promotions release owed fees too.
- The marker is `registrations.withdrawal_fee_due boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false`, with `withdrawal_kind` (EARLY, LATE, BANNED) and `withdrawn_at`. It means "a late-withdrawal fee is currently owed". The database lets it become true only in the UPDATE that enters WITHDRAWN with kind LATE, and afterwards only true to false (D-63, D-64). The partial index `(session_id, withdrawn_at) WHERE withdrawal_fee_due` (D-67) serves the oldest-first release read and the widened D-85 predicate (section 7).
- Re-registration reuses the one row `UNIQUE(session_id, user_id)` allows and replaces its guests. While a fee is owed, a re-registration is allowed only into a CONFIRMED place for a group at least as large; otherwise it is refused with 409 `late_withdrawal_fee_due`, and nothing is written.
- An admin capacity cut can leave a liability standing for a seat that no longer exists. The admin chose fewer places and the player did withdraw late. The rule is uniform and is noted for review with billing and admin reporting.

### 5. The registrant's pricing snapshot (D-62, D-63; ADR 0017 C-PRICE-1, R15)

`registrations.registrant_skill_level` and `registrations.registrant_sex` are written by `repo.ts` in the same statement that makes a row CONFIRMED, on all four transitions into CONFIRMED (register, promotion, auto-enrol, a confirmed re-registration). They are read from `team_members.skill_level` and `users.sex` under the `FOR SHARE` lock the member gate already holds, so no new lock is needed. A CHECK requires both to be non-NULL when a row is CONFIRMED or its fee is owed, and the transition guard (D-64) forbids changing them except on a transition into CONFIRMED. The fee estimate shown before confirmation uses the current level and names it. The level used for the charge is the one at confirmation.

### 6. Auto-enrol (ADR 0017 C-SKL-1; ADR 0019 section 6)

- `autoEnrolInSession(ctx, { teamId, sessionId, entries })` takes the entries in `selectAutoEnrollees` order. It runs in SSE's follow-up transaction (`runSessionAutoEnrol`), whose first statement is SSE's advisory lock. This replaces the "inside the materialisation transaction" wording of the original C-SAE-1 (ARCH line 590).
- The gate step runs `team_members FOR SHARE` for every entry, in caller order, before the one session lock. Under that row lock the allocator re-checks `is_auto_enroll`, `auto_enroll_until IS NULL OR auto_enroll_until >= game_sessions.date`, and `auto_enroll_assigned_at = entry.assignedAt` at millisecond precision. A mismatch is the returned skip `auto_enroll_changed` (R16).
- Members with three or more OVERDUE payments, or no longer APPROVED, are skipped without a row. A party never has guests.
- An existing row in ANY status, WITHDRAWN of any kind included, is skipped as `already_registered`, checked under the session lock after the member gates and before the decision. The row is not re-activated, relabelled or emailed (ADR 0019 section 8 (b), ALG R-b, R21). A retried durable job, or a sweep after a member withdrew, therefore never puts them back. `late_withdrawal_fee_due` cannot arise here, so it is not a port reason.
- Placements are labelled `source = 'AUTO_ENROL'` (D-62) and one email is queued in the placing transaction per placed member, never for a skip (key `auto-enrol:<registrationId>:<registered_at ISO>`). A panel register or re-register is always `MEMBER`.
- P-1 (ADR 0019 section 6): Auto Enroll applies to every published session, one-off or series. On the first worker boot after the real adapter is wired (section 9), SSE's boot sweep enrols every PUBLISHED, not-started session that was left pending, and sends one email per placed member per session in that single run. Operators should expect that burst at deploy. ADR 0019 accepts it as the bounded exception to ADR 0017 point 7.

### 7. D-85: the fee lock, the display count and the O1 split (D-68)

ADR 0019 section 8 (a) asks this feature to widen SSE's predicate `game_session_has_active_registrations` to also count a WITHDRAWN row with `withdrawal_fee_due`, so a late fee that is still owed keeps a session's fees locked. The migration does this with `CREATE OR REPLACE` (D-68). Its down migration restores SSE's original body and never drops the function, because `game_sessions_fee_raise_locked` depends on it. The TypeScript twin `countActiveRegistrations` in `apps/api/src/modules/sessions/repo.ts` widens in the same change, and the parity test `sessions-parity.int.test.ts` fails if the two drift (AC-VERIFY-12).

**O1 ruling (human, 2026-10-24): split.** One count cannot serve both jobs:

- `countActiveRegistrations` (the fee lock) counts CONFIRMED, WAITLISTED and fee-due WITHDRAWN rows. It drives only `feesLocked`, the 409 `session_fee_locked`, and the series `fee_locked` skip.
- `countRegisteredPlayers` (display) counts only CONFIRMED and WAITLISTED rows, one per registration. It drives every "{n} players are registered" line: the edit and fee-lock banners, the cancel and end-series counts, and the list and series rows.

The rejected alternatives were one widened count for everything, which would tell an admin that "1 player is registered" when that person withdrew and merely owes a fee, and seat counting with guests, which was not adopted. Every SSE call site is classified once in the task file (R20 table) and was switched in Phase 1. One copy edge is left to SSE: a session whose only liable row is a fee-due withdrawal shows "No one has registered yet." beside the fee-lock message. The fee-lock wording was reworded to "a player has registered or still owes a late-withdrawal fee", and the series lines follow it.

### 8. Contracts honoured, and the lock order inside SSE's transactions

**The lock order** is ADR 0019's `LOCK_ORDER` (`apps/api/src/modules/sessions/locks.ts`):

```
teams -> recurring_session_rules -> team_members -> game_sessions -> registrations -> registration_guests -> payments
```

Registration code uses a slice of it. It never locks `recurring_session_rules`, never takes a `teams` lock (so never after a session lock, ADR 0016), takes `team_members` only `FOR SHARE` through the gate and before any session lock, takes `game_sessions` only `FOR UPDATE` (D-14, one row per transaction), and registrations and guests after that. It takes no advisory lock: `SESSION_AUTO_ENROL_LOCK_NAMESPACE` (`0x5345_4153`) is SSE's alone and must not be reused here. READ COMMITTED throughout. `registerInSession` reads the debt count with no lock, then gates the member, then locks the session; `withdrawFromSession` and `promoteWaitlist` take the session lock first and no member lock. A deferred capacity trigger (D-65) is the database backstop: it takes the session row `FOR UPDATE NOWAIT`, so a writer that skipped the allocator fails 55P03 at once instead of forming a registration-to-session wait cycle, and it never blocks a capacity cut or a withdrawal.

**Port calls run in the caller's transaction** (R22). The adapter `createRegistrationsSessionAllocator` (`apps/api/src/modules/registrations/session-allocator-adapter.ts`) is a thin wrapper. It adds `name: 'registrations'`, `enrols: true`, the `trx.isTransaction` assertion and an exhaustive mapping of results to the port's types. It opens no transaction, sets no `lock_timeout`, does not retry and reads no clock. SSE's policies apply: its HTTP policy (2 s, three attempts) for the session `PUT` and its job policy (5 s, three attempts) for the follow-up. Every port call is safe to run again when SSE retries the whole transaction, because a retried attempt starts from rolled-back state (proved with an injected deadlock in `registrations-adapter.int.test.ts`). The module's own policies (R5: 2 s HTTP, 5 s job, three attempts, `now` read once) apply only to this module's own transactions (register, withdraw, the ban handler). The adapter imports only `allocator-port.ts` from the sessions module (`boundaries.test.ts`).

### 9. The adapter and its wiring (R19; ADR 0019 section 8)

The port is SSE's (`allocator-port.ts`), the adapter is this feature's, and `createSessionAllocator` (`apps/api/src/session-allocator.ts`) stays the single composition point. Phase 2 builds the adapter and proves it by injecting it into SSE's own code paths: `runSessionAutoEnrol`, the session `PUT` and series propagation (`registrations-adapter.int.test.ts`). **The swap landed in Phase 3.** The body of `createSessionAllocator` now returns `createRegistrationsSessionAllocator(createAllocator({ jobs, webBaseUrl }))` in place of the no-op, and `session-allocator.test.ts` proves both entrypoints call it. The swap waited for Phase 3 so that the branch never enrols anyone before both the promotion email and the auto-enrol email exist. No environment variable selects the adapter. `AppDeps.sessionAllocator` and `RegisterJobsOptions.sessionAllocator` are already required, and `jobs` is the API producer or the worker runner, so emails go on the caller's transaction either way. The real-adapter marker proof (a pending session enrolled once and stamped, a second sweep enrolling nobody) is AC-INTEGRATION-6, whose domain half is Phase 2 and whose end-to-end half is Phase 3.

### 10. Route ownership, O2 and O3 (R23, R24)

**Route namespace.** Reserved for this feature: `GET /teams/:teamId/sessions/upcoming` and `/teams/:teamId/sessions/:sessionId/registration*`. SSE's `GET /teams/:teamId/sessions/:sessionId` is `TEAM_ADMIN`, so the static `upcoming` segment must win over SSE's parametric route (tested in Phase 3). The player page `/teams/[teamId]/sessions/[sessionId]` and the "Upcoming sessions" section are this feature's. SSE's admin pages stay under `/teams/[teamId]/manage/sessions`, and only the registration panel changes a registration.

**O2 ruling (human, 2026-10-24): another team's session id stays 404 on registration's routes.** ADR 0019 (e) answers 403 `forbidden` for an id that exists under another team and 404 for an id that exists nowhere, using a lock-free `SELECT id` by id alone on the failure path of SSE's admin routes. This feature's member-facing routes answer 404 `not_found` with an identical body for another team's id, a DRAFT id and an unknown id (AC-ENTRY-2). The difference is deliberate. SSE's routes are `TEAM_ADMIN` routes: a caller who administers a team may be told an id exists elsewhere. A member-facing route is reachable by every member, and a 403 would tell a member that a session id exists in a team they cannot see. The 404 rule was approved with the spec. Registration's service therefore never calls `sessionExists` or any lookup by id alone: every read is scoped by `(sessionId, teamId)`. A test in `registrations-api.int.test.ts` (Phase 3) asserts the identical 404 body for all three cases.

**Read-model `blockedReason` precedence (Phase 3).** `GET .../registration` reports the first reason that stops a new registration, in this order: `session_not_open`, `already_registered`, `overdue_payments` (3 or more), `party_exceeds_capacity` (no public place). A fee-due withdrawn caller is not blocked here; whether the group fits is the allocator's answer. The POST keeps its own order (the member gate first, so a debtor gets 403 `overdue_payments` even on a closed session), which is why the page and the POST can differ for a closed session with debt. The rule lives in `access.ts`.

**Static route precedence (Phase 3).** `registrationsRoutes` is registered before `sessionsRoutes` in `app.ts`. `upcoming` is a static segment and wins over SSE's `/:sessionId` regardless, and the registration routes are the only ones under `/:sessionId/registration*`. The order records intent; the test in Phase 3 guards it.

**O3 ruling (human, 2026-10-24): accept and document; no `expectedSessionVersion`.** The register body carries no session-version field, and there is no 409 `session_changed`. Before the first registration an admin can still raise fees or change times, so a player can register on terms that differ from the page they loaded. The trade-off is bounded: the window is only the first registrant, or the first few, racing an admin edit. Once anyone is registered, or a late withdrawer still owes a fee, D-85 stops fee raises (`game_sessions_fee_raise_locked`, 409 `session_fee_locked`). The fee line on the page is an estimate. A deadline that moves while the withdraw confirmation is open is covered separately: the withdraw body is strict `{ expectLate: boolean }`, and a LATE classification inside the transaction with `expectLate` false is refused with 409 `cancellation_terms_changed` (R9), so the page re-opens the confirmation with the late wording. Not adopted from ADR 0019's conventions, because they do not fit these routes: `feesLocked` (an admin edit-form concern), explicit `changed` flags on write responses (a repeat register or withdraw is a 409 `already_registered` or `not_registered`), and `expectedVersion` (no registration route edits a versioned row).

### 11. The ban handler and its sweep (ARCH Q4, R6, O4)

One job per membership, one transaction per session through `withdrawFromSession({ cause: 'ban' })`, continuing past a failed session, with a transient-first failure classification. The sweep runs at `45 5 * * *` Pacific/Auckland (moved from 05:30 by ruling O4, because `skill-progression` already holds 05:30 as the last daily slot and 05:45 is after the 02:00 to 02:59 DST window), once at worker start, and on demand, capped at 1000 memberships. The handler never emails the banned member. The worker pool grows from 2 to 5 connections. This is built in Phase 4.

### 12. Rollback order

This feature widens a function SSE owns (D-85, D-68) and, once Phase 3 ships, replaces the allocator body SSE composes. **Rolling session-scheduling-engine back therefore requires rolling this feature back first.** The down migration restores SSE's original `game_session_has_active_registrations` body and never drops it, because SSE's trigger depends on it and SSE's own down migration drops it. In a rollback, roll registration back before SSE, and never the other way round, or the function SSE needs at its own rollback is not in the state its migration expects.

## Alternatives considered

- **Block (strict head-of-line).** In simulation it leaves four to eight times more seats empty while a large party waits, and charges late withdrawers although someone was waiting. Bounded skip has block's costs plus persisted counters and arbitrary knobs. Best-fit packing breaks FIFO among the parties that fit.
- **Liability decided once at withdrawal.** In simulation 69 to 86 percent of liable seats were resold before the start, so they would be charged twice. Settlement at session end inside billing duplicates the rule across features and cannot express partial coverage. Proportional per-seat liability needs arbitrary guest selection and partial payments for about two to three seats per 100 sessions. A liability table or derivation from history gains nothing over a column, because there is one payment per registration, or is fragile.
- **A single widened count for the fee lock and the display (O1 option a).** Rejected as in section 7.
- **Wiring the adapter in Phase 2.** Rejected: the branch would enrol members before the emails exist (section 9).
- **Registration answering 403 for another team's id (O2).** Rejected as in section 10.
- **`expectedSessionVersion` on register (O3).** Rejected as in section 10.
- **A migration-time `DROP` and re-create of the D-85 function.** Rejected: SSE's trigger depends on it.

## Consequences

- A party of three can be passed over by smaller parties. This is by design and the panel explains it.
- A late withdrawer's outcome is provisional until the start. The panel shows the current state, and a withdrawal's cost is an estimate until billing charges it.
- Rounding favours players on mismatched party sizes.
- **Accepted correctness gap (C3), needs human acknowledgement before merge: a ban can commit concurrently with a promotion.** The promotion's `team_members` read is a plain join with no lock, because locking `team_members` after the session lock would violate the canonical lock order (ADR 0015, ADR 0016), which this feature does not change. A member banned in that window can be promoted to CONFIRMED and receive one promotion email they should not. The consequence is bounded: the Phase 4 ban handler then withdraws the row with kind BANNED and no fee (proved for a CONFIRMED promoted row, any time), and billing reads the hand-off at or after `starts_at`, after the sweep, so a banned member is never billed.
- **Deferred optimisation (M2):** `autoEnrolInSession` reloads the session state for each member it places (about six queries per member, the session lock held for the whole batch). Batches are small and each placement changes the state the next one needs, so this is accepted for now; carrying the state forward through the batch is a known, deferred optimisation.
- A banned member's waitlisted row can sit in the queue, holding its position, until the ban handler withdraws it. It is never promoted meanwhile (section 2).
- On the first worker boot after the adapter ships, operators will see one auto-enrol email per placed member per pending session (P-1). Each session is stamped once, in the transaction that places its members, so the burst happens once.
- Each consumer's plan must cite C-SSE, C-SAE, C-BIL and C-ALL here. The build gate of this feature re-runs `sessions-contracts.int.test.ts` and the D-85 parity test.
- The cancelled-session state is visible only on the page (P-2). A cancelled session with CONFIRMED rows is never billed, because billing bills PUBLISHED to COMPLETED only.
- Two encodings of the transition rules exist (SQL guard and the TypeScript decision). A shared scenario matrix runs against both, so they cannot drift unnoticed.
